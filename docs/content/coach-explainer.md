# The Coach's explainer

Onboarding, in the Coach's voice. Shown once after the country is built and
before the try-out, and available any time from the Coach button.

## Rules for this copy

- **Written for a 10-year-old, not down to one.** Short sentences, concrete
  nouns, no jargon. Say "your card" not "your competency profile".
- **Six screens, hard maximum.** He will not read eight. Each screen is one
  idea and fits on a phone without scrolling.
- **Skippable from screen one**, and re-readable later. A child who wants to
  play should be allowed to play.
- **Never mentions rating numbers, Elo, or standards.** Those exist; they are
  not what he needs to hear on day one.
- **The last screen is the important one.** It is the whole philosophy of the
  thing, and it is the one that has to land.

---

## Screen 1 — Welcome

> Right. You're the manager, the captain and the whole squad.
>
> Your country is on the map now, and nobody has heard of you yet. That's the
> fun part — everyone starts there. Brazil started there.
>
> Let's find out what you can do.

*[Start the try-out]* · *[Skip — I know how this works]*

---

## Screen 2 — The try-out

> First, a scouting session. About twenty-four questions, ten minutes, no
> pressure and no score.
>
> It's not a test. I'm just working out what you're already good at so I don't
> stick you in matches that are too easy or too hard.
>
> Some of it will be stuff you know cold. Some of it won't. Both are useful to
> me — get the ones you can and don't sweat the rest.

---

## Screen 3 — Your card

> Afterwards you get a card, like the ones in FC. Six numbers:
>
> - **PAC** — how fast you know things without stopping to work them out
> - **SHO** — shapes and angles
> - **PAS** — word problems, the ones with a few steps
> - **DRI** — fractions
> - **DEF** — big numbers, place value, carrying and borrowing
> - **PHY** — measuring: length, weight, time, area
>
> Every country has a card too. Before a match you can look at theirs. If
> Brazil's DRI is 92 and yours is 58, you know exactly where they're going to
> come at you — and exactly what to go practise.

---

## Screen 4 — How a match works

> Fourteen questions. Get one right and you move up the pitch. Get to the top
> and you take your shot.
>
> Miss one and the ball comes loose — but it's not gone. I'll throw you a
> smaller question to win it back. Get that and you're straight back on the
> original one, and now you've got the missing piece.
>
> Near misses count for something. If the answer's 42 and you say 43, that's a
> shot on target — the keeper just got a hand to it. Different from one that
> sails into the stands, and I'll tell you which it was.

---

## Screen 5 — Getting to the World Cup

> You start ranked about 60th in the world. Nobody's afraid of you yet.
>
> Friendlies first, then qualifiers, then the World Cup. Win one and you get a
> star above your crest, forever. Brazil have five. Italy have four and didn't
> even make it this time — that's how hard it is.
>
> You can win your first one soon. You don't need to know everything to lift a
> trophy; you need to be solid at what you know. New stuff will show up through
> the year as you're ready for it, and it only ever opens up new tournaments —
> it never locks you out of the ones you've earned.

---

## Screen 6 — The one that matters

> Two things, then I'll leave you alone.
>
> **You can lose a match and still climb the rankings.** Play well against a
> side better than you and you go *up*, even in defeat. That's not me being
> nice — that's how world rankings actually work.
>
> **Taking the hard shot counts even when it misses.** When you're in front of
> goal you can play it safe or you can go for the bicycle kick. If you go for
> it and it doesn't come off, I'll still be clapping. That's the bit I care
> about most, and I'm keeping score of it separately.
>
> Nobody's good at this on day one. Come on.

*[Let's go]*

---

## The Coach elsewhere in the game

Same voice, much shorter. In-match tips are one or two sentences and appear
between questions, never during one:

- *"You're slow on nines. Try ten groups minus one — nine sevens is seventy
  take seven."*
- *"Unlike denominators? Check whether the small one goes into the big one
  first. Saves you a step."*
- *"That's three fraction questions they've won. Go and drill it before the
  next one."*

Never scolds. Never says "wrong". Names the thing to try next.
